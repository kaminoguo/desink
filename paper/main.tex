\begin{abstract}
Effective rank, RankMe, the leading eigenvalue share $E_1$, CKA and anisotropy are used to describe how transformer representations change during pretraining. We show that one direction fixes the value of each. For any statistic that takes the exponential entropy of a normalized spectrum, removing the top eigendirection $\bs$ from the hidden states $\bH$ gives the identity $M(\bH) = e^{\hb(t)}M(\bC)^{1-t}$, where $\bC$ is the residual and $t$ is the share of the spectrum that $\bs$ carries. On 4{,}743 measurements spanning Pythia at 160M, 410M and 1.4B, OLMo-2 1B, every layer and 20 checkpoints, this reproduces effective rank to $3\times10^{-15}$ relative error. Because effective rank normalizes eigenvalues while RankMe normalizes singular values, the two receive different shares, and we prove that RankMe's is never the larger of the two. That ordering decides the damage: at the peak layer of Pythia-1.4B a single direction holds 85 percent of the eigenvalue mass but 6 percent of the singular-value mass, and distorts the two statistics by $243\times$ and $1.24\times$. The direction is the attention sink, which acquires that role during training, ending with roughly half its energy on the $1/512$ of tokens at position~0; its strength alone accounts for 94 to 97 percent of the variance in $E_1$. Projecting it out turns a $105\times$ collapse in effective rank into $2.1\times$ growth, leaves the raw RankMe trajectory pointing the wrong way at two of three scales, and leaves one early transient that OLMo-2 reproduces. OLMo-2 grows no sink over its first 76B tokens, and there raw and residual metrics agree to 3.5 percent, which separates the sink from the projection used to study it. Because entropy-based ranks have not converged at 524{,}288 tokens, we report ratios at a fixed token count and never an absolute rank.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Effective rank, RankMe, the leading eigenvalue share $E_1$, CKA and anisotropy are computed from one $n \times d$ matrix of token representations, and all of them have been read as descriptions of how a transformer organizes information over training. \citet{li2025tracing} use RankMe and a power-law spectral exponent to report three geometric phases in pretraining. The individual statistics were established earlier: anisotropy by \citet{ethayarajh2019contextual}, rogue dimensions by \citet{timkey2021all}, RankMe as a label-free proxy for downstream quality by \citet{garrido2023rankme}, and layer comparison by CKA in \citet{raghu2021vision}.

Those same matrices are the subject of a second literature, which reports that a few coordinates carry variance orders of magnitude above the rest. \citet{xiao2024efficient} named the attention sink and \citet{sun2024massive} measured the massive activations behind it. \citet{gu2025attention} traced when the sink appears during training, and \citet{queipodellano2025sinks} proved that massive activations force the effective rank of a layer down. The two literatures rarely cite each other.

\paragraph{Problem.} Fix a checkpoint, a layer, and a corpus of $n$ tokens, and let $\bH \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times d}$ hold the hidden states. Let $M(\bH)$ be a spectral summary. Given a trajectory $\{M(\bH_t)\}$ over training steps $t$, decide which features of the trajectory describe the distribution of the $n$ representations and which describe the size of a few outlier coordinates.

Write $\bs$ for the top eigenvector of the covariance of $\bH$, and $\bC = \bH(\bI - \bs\bs^\top)$ for the residual after $\bs$ is projected out. Every statistic in the list above that takes the exponential entropy of a normalized spectrum satisfies
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:teaser}
M(\bH) \;=\; e^{\hb(t)}\cdot M(\bC)^{\,1 - t},
\end{equation}
with $\hb$ the binary entropy in nats and $t$ the share of that spectrum carried by $\bs$. This is an identity. It holds for every matrix and constrains nothing about $\bC$. A study that reports effective rank falling from $442$ to $4.2$ has reported $t$ and $M(\bC)$, and nothing else.

Which $t$ enters depends on the statistic. Effective rank normalizes eigenvalues, so $t = E_1 = \lambda_1/\sum_i\lambda_i$; RankMe normalizes singular values, so $t = \sigma_1/\sum_i\sigma_i$. Lemma~\ref{lem:order} shows the second is never the larger of the two. One direction can therefore ruin effective rank and leave RankMe close to its residual value, which is the split Section~\ref{sec:dynamics} finds in Pythia. The residue left in RankMe is small. It is also enough to reverse the sign of the RankMe trajectory at two of the three scales we measured.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
\item Section~\ref{sec:algebra} proves the identity for effective rank, RankMe, anisotropy and CKA, and the ordering $r \le p$ between the two rank shares. The effective-rank instance reproduces measurement to $3\times10^{-15}$ on 4{,}743 records and the ordering is never violated.
\item The ordering predicts a split that Table~\ref{tab:split} confirms. One direction at the peak layer of Pythia-1.4B takes $85$ percent of the eigenvalue mass and $6$ percent of the singular-value mass, and distorts the two statistics by $243\times$ and $1.24\times$.
\item The distortion ratio is bounded above by the residual value, which grows with model width. Distortion factors quoted for models of different sizes are therefore not comparable, and the $700\times$ figures in the literature are partly a statement about $d$.
\item Across Pythia at three scales the direction ends up carrying about half its energy on the $1/512$ of tokens at position~0, and its strength alone accounts for $94$ to $97$ percent of the variance in $E_1$. Raw effective rank falls by two orders of magnitude while the residual grows.
\item OLMo-2 1B forms no sink in its first 76B tokens. There raw and residual metrics agree to a median of $3.5$ percent, which is the control the argument needs: the gap follows sink strength and not the projection.
\end{enumerate}

The propositions in Section~\ref{sec:algebra} are elementary. Their consequence is not. A class of published measurements turns out to carry no information beyond two other numbers, and one of the five statistics survives the problem far better than the others.

\section{Setting}
\label{sec:setting}

\paragraph{Hidden states.} Fix a model, a layer, and a corpus. Running the corpus through the model yields $n$ token representations in $\mathbb{R}^d$, stacked as the rows of $\bH \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times d}$. Write $\bar{\bH} = (\bI_n - \tfrac{1}{n}\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^\top)\bH$ for the column-centered matrix, $\bSig = \tfrac{1}{n}\bar{\bH}^\top\bar{\bH}$ for the covariance, and $\lambda_1 \ge \dots \ge \lambda_d \ge 0$ for its eigenvalues with eigenvectors $\bv_1,\dots,\bv_d$. Set $V = \tr\bSig$ and $\sigma_i = \sqrt{n\lambda_i}$ for the singular values of $\bar{\bH}$.

\paragraph{Spectral summaries.} For a nonzero $\bw \in \mathbb{R}^m_{\ge 0}$ define the exponential entropy
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:hexp}
\Hexp(\bw) \;=\; \exp\Big(-\sum_{i=1}^{m} \tilde{w}_i \log \tilde{w}_i\Big), \qquad \tilde{w}_i = w_i / \textstyle\sum_j w_j ,
\end{equation}
with $0\log 0 = 0$. The five summaries this paper studies are
\begin{align}
E_1(\bH) &= \lambda_1 / V , &
\ER(\bH) &= \Hexp(\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_d) , &
\RankMe(\bH) &= \Hexp(\sigma_1,\dots,\sigma_d) , \label{eq:defs1}
\end{align}
\begin{equation}
\rho(\bH) = \frac{1}{n(n-1)}\sum_{i \ne j} \frac{\langle \bH_i, \bH_j\rangle}{\|\bH_i\|\,\|\bH_j\|}, \qquad
\CKA(\bX,\bY) = \frac{\|\bX^\top \bY\|_F^2}{\|\bX^\top\bX\|_F \, \|\bY^\top\bY\|_F} .
\label{eq:defs2}
\end{equation}
Here $\bH_i$ is the $i$-th row of $\bH$, $\rho$ is the anisotropy of \citet{ethayarajh2019contextual}, and $\CKA$ is linear centered kernel alignment \citep{kornblith2019similarity} for column-centered $\bX \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times d_1}$ and $\bY \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times d_2}$. Effective rank \citep{roy2007effective} and RankMe \citep{garrido2023rankme} apply the same functional to different inputs: the training-dynamics literature feeds $\Hexp$ normalized eigenvalues and the self-supervised-learning literature feeds it normalized singular values. We keep both conventions and label them separately, because Section~\ref{sec:algebra} shows the choice decides how much damage one direction can do.

\paragraph{The projection.} Define
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:proj}
\bC \;=\; \bH\,(\bI_d - \bs\bs^\top), \qquad \bs = \bv_1 .
\end{equation}
We call $\bC$ the \emph{residual} and $\bs$ the \emph{leading direction}. Removing $\bs$ from a matrix of embeddings is the All-but-the-Top postprocessing of \citet{mu2018allbutthetop} with one component; \citet{li2020sentence} and \citet{rajaee2021cluster} use related corrections to improve downstream sentence similarity. This paper uses the projection as a measurement control rather than as a way to improve a downstream score.

\paragraph{Sink strength.} For a corpus of $B$ sequences of length $T$, let $\alpha$ be the mean norm of the hidden states at sequence position~$0$ divided by the mean norm at all other positions. An attention sink at this layer makes $\alpha$ large \citep{xiao2024efficient,sun2024massive}. We also report the \emph{position-0 mass} of the leading direction, the fraction of $\sum_i \langle \bar{\bH}_i, \bs\rangle^2$ contributed by rows at position~$0$.

\paragraph{Models, corpora, and protocol.} The training-dynamics measurements use Pythia at 160M, 410M and 1.4B with the 20 released checkpoints from step~0 to step~143{,}000 \citep{biderman2023pythia}, and the 13 released stage-1 checkpoints of OLMo-2 1B \citep{olmo2025olmo2}. Every checkpoint sees the same 606{,}208 OpenWebText tokens \citep{gokaslan2019openwebtext} in sequences of 512. We split them into three disjoint blocks of 200{,}704 tokens and treat the blocks as independent samples. The token array carries 28{,}552 distinct ids, no duplicated sequences, and a published SHA-256 prefix. Weights are loaded in float32 and all layers are measured. Cross-block spread at the peak layers is $0.1$ to $1.9$ percent, and every figure shows it. Section~\ref{sec:scope} adds final-checkpoint measurements of GPT-2 and GPT-2-XL \citep{radford2019language}, Pythia-6.9B, and Qwen2.5-1.5B \citep{qwen2024qwen25} on WikiText-2 \citep{merity2017pointer} and OpenWebText from an earlier round; Table~\ref{tab:scope} lists the corpus and token count behind every number in the paper. Total compute is under 40 GPU-hours.

\section{One direction fixes the value of each metric}
\label{sec:algebra}

\subsection{An entropy split and an ordering}

\begin{lemma}[Top-entry split]
\label{lem:split}
Let $\bw \in \mathbb{R}^m_{\ge 0}$ with $\|\bw\|_1 > 0$, let $w_1 = \max_i w_i$, let $t = w_1/\|\bw\|_1 < 1$, and let $\bw_{-1}$ be $\bw$ with its first entry deleted. Then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:split}
\Hexp(\bw) \;=\; e^{\hb(t)} \cdot \Hexp(\bw_{-1})^{\,1-t}, \qquad \hb(t) = -t\log t - (1-t)\log(1-t).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

Lemma~\ref{lem:split} restates the chain rule for Shannon entropy. Everything below follows from it and from one observation: centering acts on the left and the projection in Equation~\ref{eq:proj} acts on the right, so $\bar{\bC} = \bar{\bH}(\bI - \bs\bs^\top)$, the covariance of $\bC$ equals $\bSig - \lambda_1 \bs\bs^\top$, and its spectrum is $\{\lambda_2,\dots,\lambda_d, 0\}$.

\begin{lemma}[Share ordering]
\label{lem:order}
Let $p = \lambda_1/\sum_i \lambda_i$ and $r = \sigma_1/\sum_i \sigma_i$ with $\sigma_i = \sqrt{n\lambda_i}$. Then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:order}
\frac{p}{r} \;=\; \frac{\sigma_1 \sum_i \sigma_i}{\sum_i \sigma_i^2} \;\ge\; 1 ,
\end{equation}
with equality if and only if every nonzero singular value equals $\sigma_1$, that is, the spectrum is flat on its support.
\end{lemma}

\subsection{The identities}

\begin{proposition}[Leading share]
\label{prop:e1}
Let $x = \lambda_1 / \tr(\bSig - \lambda_1\bs\bs^\top)$. Then $E_1(\bH) = x/(x+1)$, a strictly increasing bijection from $[0,\infty)$ onto $[0,1)$. The value of $E_1(\bH)$ carries exactly the ratio of leading to residual variance, and nothing about the shape of the residual spectrum.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Effective rank]
\label{prop:er}
With $p = E_1(\bH) < 1$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:er}
\ER(\bH) \;=\; e^{\hb(p)} \cdot \ER(\bC)^{\,1-p} .
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[RankMe]
\label{prop:rankme}
With $r = \sigma_1/\sum_i \sigma_i$, $\ \RankMe(\bH) = e^{\hb(r)}\cdot\RankMe(\bC)^{\,1-r}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{corollary}[Distortion, and which metric it reaches]
\label{cor:bound}
Write $D_M = M(\bC)/M(\bH)$. Then $D_M = M(\bC)^{t}\,e^{-\hb(t)}$ with $t$ the share belonging to $M$, so
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:bound}
D_M \le M(\bC), \qquad D_M \to M(\bC) \text{ as } t \to 1 ,
\end{equation}
and $D_M > 1$ exactly when $t\log M(\bC) > \hb(t)$. Since $r \le p$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:order} and $D$ increases in $t$ once $D>1$, RankMe is never distorted more than effective rank at comparable residual ranks.
\end{corollary}

Corollary~\ref{cor:bound} settles how to read a distortion figure. A study that removes the leading direction from a $d = 4096$ model and reports a $700\times$ change in effective rank has reported a large $p$ and a residual rank near $10^3$. The same $p$ in a $d = 512$ model cannot produce a factor above a few hundred, because $D$ never exceeds $\ER(\bC)$. Cross-model comparisons of distortion factors compare model width as much as they compare contamination.

\begin{proposition}[Anisotropy]
\label{prop:aniso}
Let $\hat{\bu}_i = \bH_i/\|\bH_i\|$ and $\beta_i = \langle \hat{\bu}_i, \bs\rangle$. Then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:aniso}
\rho(\bH) \;=\; \frac{\big\|\sum_i \hat{\bu}_i\big\|^2 - n}{n(n-1)}
\;=\; \frac{\big(\sum_i \beta_i\big)^2 + \big\|\sum_i \hat{\bu}_i^{\perp}\big\|^2 - n}{n(n-1)},
\end{equation}
where $\hat{\bu}_i^\perp = \hat{\bu}_i - \beta_i \bs$. Removing $\bs$ deletes the first term. That term reaches $n^2$ when the $\beta_i$ share a sign and magnitude, and it is one of two, so the decomposition bounds the sink's contribution without predicting that it dominates.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[CKA homogenization]
\label{prop:cka}
Let $\bX = \bu\mathbf{p}^\top + \bX_c$ and $\bY = \bu\mathbf{q}^\top + \bY_c$ be column-centered with $\bX_c^\top\bu = \bY_c^\top\bu = \mathbf{0}$, and set $A = \|\bu\|^2\|\mathbf{p}\|^2$, $B = \|\bu\|^2\|\mathbf{q}\|^2$. Then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cka}
1 - \CKA(\bX,\bY) \;=\; O\big(A^{-1/2} + B^{-1/2}\big) ,
\end{equation}
with constants depending only on $\|\bX_c^\top\bY_c\|_F$, $\|\bX_c^\top\bX_c\|_F$, and $\|\bY_c^\top\bY_c\|_F$. Two layers sharing one high-norm rank-one term have $\CKA \to 1$ whatever their residuals do.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Specificity of the leading direction]
\label{prop:spec}
Removing the $k$-th eigendirection, $\bH_{-k} = \bH(\bI - \bv_k\bv_k^\top)$, gives $E_1(\bH_{-k}) = \lambda_1/(V-\lambda_k) \ge E_1(\bH)$ for $k \ge 2$. For $\bu$ drawn uniformly on the unit sphere, $\mathbb{E}[\bu^\top\bSig\bu] = V/d$, so a random direction removes $1/d$ of the total variance in expectation.
\end{proposition}

Table~\ref{tab:split} reads the split off the converged checkpoints. At Pythia-1.4B the leading direction holds $85.3$ percent of the eigenvalue mass and $5.8$ percent of the singular-value mass. Table~\ref{tab:sink} tracks the direction across checkpoints. At initialization it places a tenth of a percent of its mass on position~0 and $\alpha$ sits near $1.2$, so it is ordinary content variance and $E_1(\bH)$ agrees with $E_1(\bC)$ to two digits. By the end of training the same direction carries about half its energy on the $1/512$ of tokens at position~0, a 250-fold enrichment, and $\alpha$ has reached $19.5$ at 160M and $44.7$ at 410M. $E_1(\bC)$ meanwhile never leaves the interval $[0.028, 0.048]$ in any of the three models. A residual that stays this diffuse is what makes Equation~\ref{eq:proj} a clean split; the projection is not rebalancing two components of comparable size.

\paragraph{Attention concentrates on the same position.} Norm and variance at position~0 identify a massive-activation direction; the name attention sink also asks for attention mass. Over 19 Pythia-70M checkpoints, mean attention to position~0 at L3 rises from $0.013$ to $0.135$, a factor of ten, and ranks with $\alpha$ at Spearman $0.725$ ($p = 4.5\times10^{-4}$). Both are flat until step~1{,}000 and both climb afterwards.

\paragraph{Where the identification holds.} The identification needs a spectral gap. In GPT-2 the ratio $\sigma_1/\sigma_2$ sits near $14.7$ across L2 to L4 and falls to $1.1$ at L0 and $3.0$ at L11. In Qwen2.5-1.5B it reaches $154$ at L2. Where the gap is near~1 the leading direction is not separated from content directions and $\bs$ should not be read as the sink. We report the gap alongside every layer-level claim and treat $\sigma_1/\sigma_2 > 3$ as the condition under which the identification is meaningful.

Figure~\ref{fig:dyn} tracks the peak-sink layer of each Pythia model, and two features of the raw effective-rank curve have been read as training phases. One is a drop and recovery before step~1{,}000. The other is a long decline that starts around step~4{,}000.

The early drop survives the projection. At Pythia-1.4B residual effective rank falls from $478$ to $195$ between step~16 and step~256 and then recovers, and residual $E_1$ rises to $0.096$ over the same steps before returning below $0.03$ by step~2{,}000. Raw effective rank ends the run at $4.2$, $2.3$ and $11.1$, factors of $105$, $119$ and $21$ below where it started, and the residual ends above where it started in all three models. The sign of the trajectory is opposite in the two readings, at every scale, on the same tokens.

\subsection{RankMe does not collapse and still points the wrong way}

The bottom row of Figure~\ref{fig:dyn} runs RankMe on the same matrices. Nothing collapses. Raw RankMe moves by a few percent across all of training where raw effective rank moves by two orders of magnitude, which is what Corollary~\ref{cor:bound} requires: the RankMe share sits between $0.06$ and $0.10$ at these layers, and the effective-rank share between $0.70$ and $0.91$.

A few percent is still enough to invert the reading. The right-hand columns of Table~\ref{tab:endpoints} put the raw and residual RankMe trajectories side by side. Two of the three raw curves have the wrong sign, and the third understates the residual trend by a factor of $9.4$. The disagreement is concentrated where it should be. Before step~8{,}000 the two curves are almost on top of each other; afterwards, as the sink grows, raw RankMe turns down by $9$ to $23$ percent while the residual stays flat or keeps rising. A RankMe trajectory read at face value reports a late decline that is not in the representations.

\subsection{Sink strength predicts the leading share}

Proposition~\ref{prop:e1} reduces $E_1$ to the ratio $x$ of leading to residual variance. A rank-one term of magnitude $\alpha$ contributes variance proportional to $\alpha^2$, which predicts $x = \kappa\alpha^2$ and
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:alphafit}
E_1(\bH) \;=\; \frac{\kappa\alpha^2}{\kappa\alpha^2 + 1} .
\end{equation}
We fit the single parameter $\kappa$ over the 20 checkpoints of each model, with $\alpha$ and $E_1$ taken from the same tokens. The fits give $R^2$ between $0.939$ and $0.971$, and the implied $x$ correlates with $\alpha^2$ at $r \ge 0.978$ in all three. A scalar computed from token norms, blind to the spectrum, accounts for most of a trajectory that has been read as representational reorganization.

\subsection{A model without a sink shows no gap}

Every claim above compares a raw quantity with one computed after a projection. A reader may ask how much of the gap the projection itself creates. OLMo-2 1B answers this without an intervention: over its first 76B tokens it never develops a sink. Sink strength stays below $1.273$ across all 17 layers and 13 checkpoints, and the position-0 mass of the leading direction stays below $0.012$. On those matrices raw and residual effective rank differ by a median of $3.5$ percent, and raw effective rank rises monotonically from $111$ to $1272$ after the early dip. The projection removes a direction in OLMo-2 exactly as it does in Pythia; what changes is that the direction is not carrying anything.

OLMo-2 also reproduces the early transient. Its effective rank falls from $1008$ at initialization to $111$ at step~1{,}000 with $\alpha = 1.04$, then climbs for the rest of the measured run. The sink-free rank dip appears in two architecture families.

We do not read this as evidence that OLMo-2 never forms a sink. Step~36{,}000 is about 2 percent of its schedule, Pythia's sink at these layers only becomes visible after a comparable fraction, and OLMo-2's $\alpha$ is drifting up at the last measured checkpoints. The claim supported here is that OLMo-2 has no sink over the interval we measured, and that where there is no sink there is no gap.

\subsection{Rogue dimensions and the leading direction are the same object}

\citet{timkey2021all} identified a handful of coordinates that dominate cosine similarity in transformer representations and argued that they obscure representational quality. In GPT-2 the top five such coordinates carry $87.3$ to $88.6$ percent of the leading direction's $\ell_1$ mass at layers L3 through L10, against $3.8$ percent at L0 and $20.9$ percent at L1. Table~\ref{tab:audit} gives the five summaries at the peak layer of two models.

\subsection{Anisotropy responds to the sink but is not driven by it}

Proposition~\ref{prop:aniso} splits anisotropy into a sink term and a residual term without asserting which of the two dominates. Measurement says the residual term does. Removing the leading direction at the peak-sink layers moves uncentered anisotropy by $6$ to $22$ percent, where effective rank on the same matrices moves by $32\times$ to $243\times$. Anisotropy also has dynamics the sink cannot supply. It peaks around step~16, at which point $\alpha$ is still near $1.1$, and falls for the rest of training. We therefore call anisotropy sink-sensitive, and stop short of calling it sink-driven.

\paragraph{The direction carries no probeable information where it dominates.} Table~\ref{tab:probe} reports linear probing on Pythia-70M. At L3 and L4, where the leading direction holds 63 to 65 percent of the variance, removing it changes accuracy by $0.04$ and $0.06$ points. Across L0 through L5 the largest change is $0.24$ points. A direction that holds most of the variance and none of the next-token information a linear readout can use is a scaffold for the forward pass rather than a store of content, which is the reading of \citet{barbero2025first} and \citet{zhang2025catch}.

L6, the output of the last block, is the exception and it is instructive. There the leading direction holds $0.4$ percent of the variance, the spectral gap implied by Table~\ref{tab:audit} is $1.4$, and removing it raises accuracy by $12.13$ points. By the scope rule of Section~\ref{sec:sink} that direction is not the sink. We report the number because it shows the projection doing something real at a layer where the sink identification fails, and we do not attribute the gain to the sink.

\paragraph{The sink direction has no causal privilege at position~0.} We measured perplexity on Pythia-410M after removing the leading direction from the position-0 residual stream at one layer at a time, and compared against removing a random direction of the same magnitude, over 5 trials and 12 evaluation sentences. Removing the leading direction raises perplexity by up to $49\times$ over a baseline of $20.4$, averaging $17.8\times$ across the 24 layers. The matched-random control averages $42.9\times$ and exceeds the leading direction at 13 of 24 layers. Position~0 is fragile to a perturbation in any direction. The heading of this paragraph is the finding: whatever the sink does for the forward pass, a matched-magnitude random direction does more damage, so this experiment gives the sink no special causal status.

\paragraph{Decisions that use relative layer ordering survive the contamination.} On Pythia-6.9B we ranked layers by Block Influence, the cosine distance between a layer's input and output residual stream, and pruned the 8 lowest. Pruning by raw scores gives perplexity $31.27$ and pruning by residual scores gives $33.81$, against $16.23$ for the full model. The two sets overlap in 7 of 8 layers. Distilling GPT-2 into a 6-layer student gives perplexity $5.57$ for even layer spacing, $6.31$ for raw CKA matching, and $6.01$ for residual CKA matching. Cross-model CKA between GPT-2 and Pythia-70M rises from a maximum of $0.016$ to $0.116$ after the projection, so the small cross-model similarity is not an artifact of a shared sink. The contamination distorts absolute values of spectral summaries and leaves the ordering of layers largely intact.

\paragraph{Effective rank has not converged in sample size.} Table~\ref{tab:nsweep} resamples Pythia-1.4B L5 at four corpus sizes. $E_1$, $\alpha$, and anisotropy settle to within $2$ percent by $n = 524{,}288$; effective rank and RankMe do not, and both rise monotonically with $n$. At the final checkpoint $\ER(\bC)$ moves from $756$ to $1036$ over the four sizes. This is the sample-spectrum bias of a $2048$-dimensional covariance estimated from finitely many tokens: small eigenvalues are underestimated at low $n$, so an entropy-based rank approaches its limit from below. Comparisons are valid because every point in a given figure uses the same $n$, and the raw-to-residual ratio at step~143{,}000 is $194\times$ at $n = 8{,}192$ and $246\times$ at $n = 524{,}288$, so the conclusion does not turn on the choice.

\paragraph{The OLMo-2 control covers 2 percent of a run.} Step~36{,}000 is 76B tokens. Pythia's sink at these layers becomes visible only after a comparable fraction of its schedule, and OLMo-2's $\alpha$ is rising at the last measured checkpoint. Section~\ref{sec:dynamics} claims that OLMo-2 has no sink over the measured interval, which is what a control needs, and not that OLMo-2 never forms one.

\paragraph{One protocol change across the paper.} The dynamics results use 200{,}704 OpenWebText tokens per block. The GPT-2, Pythia-70M, Pythia-6.9B, and Qwen2.5 measurements come from an earlier round with 22{,}819 to 200{,}000 tokens on WikiText-2 or OpenWebText, listed per row in Table~\ref{tab:scope}. Given Table~\ref{tab:nsweep}, effective ranks are not comparable across those rows, and we compare only within a row.

\paragraph{The position-0 perplexity experiment is small.} It uses 12 hand-written sentences on one model, which is enough to show that the matched-random control is not smaller than the treatment and not enough to characterize the causal role of the sink.

\paragraph{The projection is linear and applies at one layer at a time.} We remove one direction from one layer and recompute summaries at that layer. We do not track how the removal propagates through subsequent layers, and we do not claim that a nonlinear correction would give the same residual.

\paragraph{The identities assume the leading direction is removed.} Equations~\ref{eq:er} and~\ref{eq:bound} hold when the projected direction is the top eigenvector. Table~\ref{tab:inject} shows what happens when it is not: below $\alpha = 3$ the prediction fails by 8 percent. In practice we check $\sigma_1/\sigma_2 > 3$ before reading $\bs$ as the sink.

\paragraph{We do not evaluate whether phases exist.} Our result is that the metrics used to detect them are determined by one direction. A study that reports phases in the residual quantities is unaffected by anything here.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Spectral summaries as descriptions of training.} \citet{li2025tracing} track RankMe and a spectral exponent across pretraining and post-training in OLMo and Pythia and report three phases. \citet{garrido2023rankme} introduced RankMe as a label-free proxy for downstream quality in self-supervised learning, \citet{roy2007effective} defined effective rank, \citet{ethayarajh2019contextual} measured anisotropy in contextual embeddings, and \citet{raghu2021vision} used CKA to compare vision transformers with convolutional networks. \citet{kulkarni2026disentangling} trained 108 models and found that effective rank tracks batch size and weight decay more than it tracks performance, a confound in the training configuration rather than in the estimator. Our result is about the estimator. It also says which of these statistics is the safer choice. RankMe absorbs a $1.2$ to $1.5$ factor from the sink where effective rank absorbs $32$ to $243$, so a study that must report one number should report RankMe together with its residual.

\paragraph{Attention sinks.} \citet{xiao2024efficient} named the attention sink and used it for streaming inference; \citet{darcet2024registers} added register tokens to vision transformers for the same reason. \citet{sun2024massive} characterized massive activations and \citet{gu2025attention} showed that sinks emerge early and strengthen with scale. \citet{barbero2025first} argued that sinks limit over-mixing, \citet{cancedda2024spectral} related sink signals to the unembedding spectrum, and \citet{zhang2025catch} showed that one or two sink tags explain a large share of PCA variance in attention outputs. Table~\ref{tab:sink} adds the trajectory of the position-0 mass of the leading eigenvector, which starts at $0.1$ percent and reaches half.

\paragraph{The closest prior result.} \citet{queipodellano2025sinks} prove that massive activations force effective rank down at a layer, and read the resulting compression valley as a functional stage of the forward pass. Proposition~\ref{prop:er} sharpens that from an inequality at one layer to an identity, and Lemma~\ref{lem:order} adds which statistics inherit it. The consequence for training-dynamics measurement follows: given the share and the residual rank, effective rank has no remaining degree of freedom to carry. Where \citet{queipodellano2025sinks} ask what sinks do inside a forward pass, we ask what they do to a measurement taken across checkpoints.

\paragraph{Removing a dominant direction.} \citet{mu2018allbutthetop} remove the top principal components of word embeddings and improve similarity benchmarks; \citet{li2020sentence} and \citet{rajaee2021cluster} pursue the same goal with normalizing flows and clustering. Those papers optimize a downstream score. We use the same operation as a control and report the residual next to the raw value rather than in place of it. \citet{timkey2021all} identified the rogue dimensions that Section~\ref{sec:scope} matches to the leading direction.

%======================================================================
\section{Conclusion}

Effective rank and RankMe factor into a binary-entropy term and a power of the residual value, so a study that reports either has reported a share and a residual and no third thing. The share is not the same for the two statistics, and it is never the larger one for RankMe; that ordering is what decides how much damage a single direction can do. In Pythia-1.4B the direction takes $85$ percent of the eigenvalue mass against $6$ percent of the singular-value mass, and distorts the two by $243\times$ and $1.24\times$ respectively. It is the attention sink, and it gets there during training: by the end of the run about half its energy sits on the $1/512$ of tokens at position~0, while a model that has not yet grown one shows raw and residual metrics agreeing to $3.5$ percent. Projecting it out leaves a residual that grows over training, and a single transient before step~1{,}000 that the sink cannot account for.

Some of this translates into reporting practice. Effective rank should be quoted next to $E_1$ and $\ER(\bC)$, which determine it, and next to the spectral gap, which tells a reader whether the direction that was removed is the sink at all. Distortion factors need the residual value that bounds them and the token count they were measured at, given that entropy-based ranks have not converged at half a million tokens. If only one rank statistic can be reported, RankMe is the better choice, and it should still be reported with its residual: a $1.24\times$ offset was enough to invert two of our three trajectories. The implementation is one line, \verb|H_res = H - (H @ s)[:, None] * s|, with \verb|s| the top right singular vector of the centered \verb|H|.

\section{Proofs}
\label{app:proofs}

\subsection{Lemma~\ref{lem:split}}

\begin{proof}
Write $\tilde{w}_i = w_i/\|\bw\|_1$, so $\tilde{w}_1 = t$ and $\sum_{i \ge 2}\tilde{w}_i = 1-t$. Let $q_i = \tilde{w}_i/(1-t)$ for $i \ge 2$ be the normalization of $\bw_{-1}$. Then
\[
-\sum_{i} \tilde{w}_i \log \tilde{w}_i
= -t\log t - \sum_{i\ge2}(1-t)q_i\big[\log(1-t) + \log q_i\big]
= \hb(t) + (1-t)\Big(-\sum_{i\ge2} q_i \log q_i\Big),
\]
using $\sum_{i \ge 2} q_i = 1$. Exponentiating gives Equation~\ref{eq:split}. The case $t=1$ makes both sides equal $1$ under the convention $0\log 0 = 0$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Lemma~\ref{lem:order}}

\begin{proof}
Write $S_1 = \sum_i \sigma_i$ and $S_2 = \sum_i \sigma_i^2$. Then $p = \sigma_1^2/S_2$ and $r = \sigma_1/S_1$, so $p/r = \sigma_1 S_1 / S_2$. Since $\sigma_i \le \sigma_1$ for every $i$,
\[
S_2 = \sum_i \sigma_i^2 \le \sigma_1 \sum_i \sigma_i = \sigma_1 S_1 ,
\]
which gives $p/r \ge 1$. Equality forces $\sum_i \sigma_i(\sigma_1 - \sigma_i) = 0$, and every summand is nonnegative, so $\sigma_i \in \{0, \sigma_1\}$ for each $i$. A flat spectrum with $k$ nonzero entries gives $p = r = 1/k$, so equality holds for every such spectrum and not only for $k=1$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Spectrum of the residual}

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:resid}
With $\bC$ as in Equation~\ref{eq:proj} and $\bs = \bv_1$, the covariance of $\bC$ is $\bSig - \lambda_1\bs\bs^\top$ and its eigenvalues are $\lambda_2 \ge \dots \ge \lambda_d \ge 0$ together with one extra zero.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Centering is left multiplication by $\bI_n - \tfrac1n\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^\top$ and the projection is right multiplication by $\bI_d - \bs\bs^\top$, so the two commute and $\bar{\bC} = \bar{\bH}(\bI_d - \bs\bs^\top)$. Hence
\[
\tfrac1n\bar{\bC}^\top\bar{\bC} = (\bI-\bs\bs^\top)\bSig(\bI-\bs\bs^\top) = \bSig - \lambda_1\bs\bs^\top,
\]
where the second equality uses $\bSig\bs = \lambda_1\bs$ and $\bs^\top\bs = 1$. In the eigenbasis of $\bSig$ this matrix is diagonal with entries $0, \lambda_2, \dots, \lambda_d$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Propositions~\ref{prop:e1}, \ref{prop:er}, \ref{prop:rankme} and Corollary~\ref{cor:bound}}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:resid}, $\tr(\bSig - \lambda_1\bs\bs^\top) = V - \lambda_1$, so $x = \lambda_1/(V-\lambda_1)$ and $x/(x+1) = \lambda_1/V = E_1(\bH)$, which proves Proposition~\ref{prop:e1}; the map $x \mapsto x/(x+1)$ is strictly increasing with inverse $p \mapsto p/(1-p)$.

For Proposition~\ref{prop:er} apply Lemma~\ref{lem:split} to $\bw = (\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_d)$. Its largest entry is $\lambda_1$ and $t = \lambda_1/V = E_1(\bH) = p$. The deleted vector $\bw_{-1} = (\lambda_2,\dots,\lambda_d)$ is the nonzero part of the spectrum of the residual covariance, and appending the extra zero of Lemma~\ref{lem:resid} leaves $\Hexp$ unchanged, so $\Hexp(\bw_{-1}) = \ER(\bC)$. Substituting into Equation~\ref{eq:split} gives Equation~\ref{eq:er}. Proposition~\ref{prop:rankme} is the same argument with $\bw = (\sigma_1,\dots,\sigma_d)$ and $t = \sigma_1/\sum_i\sigma_i$, since $\sigma_i = \sqrt{n\lambda_i}$ preserves the ordering and the residual singular values are $\sigma_2,\dots,\sigma_d$.

For Corollary~\ref{cor:bound}, divide: $D_M = M(\bC)/M(\bH) = M(\bC)^{1-(1-t)}e^{-\hb(t)} = M(\bC)^{t}e^{-\hb(t)}$. Both factors are at most $M(\bC)$ and $1$ respectively for $t \in [0,1]$ and $M(\bC) \ge 1$, so $D_M \le M(\bC)$, with equality in the limit $t \to 1$ where $\hb(t) \to 0$. Taking logarithms, $D_M > 1$ if and only if $t\log M(\bC) > \hb(t)$, and $\partial_t \log D_M = \log M(\bC) - \log\frac{1-t}{t}$ is positive whenever $D_M > 1$, so $D_M$ increases in $t$ on that range.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Proposition~\ref{prop:aniso}}

\begin{proof}
Let $\hat{\bu}_i = \bH_i/\|\bH_i\|$ and $S = \sum_i \hat{\bu}_i$. Then $\|S\|^2 = \sum_{i,j}\langle\hat{\bu}_i,\hat{\bu}_j\rangle = n + \sum_{i \ne j}\langle\hat{\bu}_i,\hat{\bu}_j\rangle$, and $\rho(\bH)$ is the average of the off-diagonal terms, which gives the first equality. Decomposing $\hat{\bu}_i = \beta_i\bs + \hat{\bu}_i^\perp$ with $\langle\bs,\hat{\bu}_i^\perp\rangle = 0$ gives $\|S\|^2 = (\sum_i\beta_i)^2 + \|\sum_i\hat{\bu}_i^\perp\|^2$, the second equality. If $\beta_i = \beta$ for all $i$ then the first term is $n^2\beta^2$ and $\rho \ge (n^2\beta^2 - n)/(n(n-1)) \to \beta^2$ for large $n$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Proposition~\ref{prop:cka}}

\begin{proof}
Since $\bX_c^\top\bu = \mathbf{0}$ and $\bY_c^\top\bu = \mathbf{0}$, the cross terms vanish and $\bX^\top\bY = \|\bu\|^2\mathbf{p}\mathbf{q}^\top + \bX_c^\top\bY_c$. Write $c_{xy} = \|\bX_c^\top\bY_c\|_F$, $c_{xx} = \|\bX_c^\top\bX_c\|_F$, $c_{yy} = \|\bY_c^\top\bY_c\|_F$, and note $\|\mathbf{p}\mathbf{q}^\top\|_F = \|\mathbf{p}\|\|\mathbf{q}\|$, so $\|\,\|\bu\|^2\mathbf{p}\mathbf{q}^\top\|_F = \sqrt{AB}$. The triangle inequality gives
\[
\|\bX^\top\bY\|_F^2 \ge \big(\sqrt{AB} - c_{xy}\big)^2, \qquad
\|\bX^\top\bX\|_F \le A + c_{xx}, \qquad \|\bY^\top\bY\|_F \le B + c_{yy}.
\]
Hence
\[
\CKA(\bX,\bY) \;\ge\; \frac{AB\big(1 - c_{xy}/\sqrt{AB}\big)^2}{AB\big(1+c_{xx}/A\big)\big(1+c_{yy}/B\big)}
\;=\; 1 - O\big(A^{-1/2} + B^{-1/2}\big),
\]
expanding to first order in $1/\sqrt{A}$ and $1/\sqrt{B}$. Since $\CKA \le 1$ always, the two-sided statement follows.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Proposition~\ref{prop:spec}}

\begin{proof}
Replacing $\bs$ by $\bv_k$ in Lemma~\ref{lem:resid} gives a residual covariance with spectrum $\{\lambda_i\}_{i \ne k}$ plus a zero, whose largest entry is $\lambda_1$ for $k \ge 2$ and whose trace is $V - \lambda_k$. Hence $E_1(\bH_{-k}) = \lambda_1/(V-\lambda_k) \ge \lambda_1/V$. For $\bu$ uniform on the sphere, $\mathbb{E}[\bu\bu^\top] = \bI_d/d$, so $\mathbb{E}[\bu^\top\bSig\bu] = \tr(\bSig)/d = V/d$.
\end{proof}

\section{Reproduction}
\label{app:repro}

The submission ships the experiment scripts, the raw output of every run quoted in the paper, and \texttt{build.py}, which regenerates all four figures and writes \texttt{numbers.txt} containing every quantity in the manuscript next to the file it came from. Running \texttt{python build.py} takes under a minute and requires no GPU. The measurements use public checkpoints and total under 40 GPU-hours.

An earlier round of our own experiments measured Pythia-160M and Pythia-1.4B training dynamics through a corpus loader whose exception handler silently substituted 200 copies of one template sentence. The resulting effective ranks were near 10 at initialization for a 768-dimensional model, which is the signature of roughly ten distinct token types. Those runs are excluded, every number derived from them is withdrawn, and Sections~\ref{sec:sink} and~\ref{sec:dynamics} report the replacement measurements on the corpus described in Section~\ref{sec:setting}. The corpus is published with its SHA-256 prefix, its distinct-token count, and a check for duplicated sequences; the shipped scripts raise on a corpus-loading failure and refuse to run when a measured effective rank at initialization falls below $d/25$.

This work identifies a measurement bias in a class of published analyses of transformer training. Its effect is on the accuracy of scientific claims about representation geometry. We see no direct societal risk from the method or from its release.
