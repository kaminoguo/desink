[1] Title*        (TeX supported; none needed)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Spectral Metrics of Transformer Training Are Determined by a Single Direction


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[2] Abstract*     (TeX supported, and this uses it. Paste verbatim.)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Effective rank, RankMe, the leading eigenvalue share $E_1$, CKA and anisotropy are used to describe how transformer representations change during pretraining. We show that one direction fixes the value of the rank statistics among them. For any statistic that takes the exponential entropy of a normalized spectrum, removing the top eigendirection $\mathbf{s}$ from the hidden states $\mathbf{H}$ gives the identity $M(\mathbf{H}) = e^{h_{\mathrm{b}}(t)}M(\mathbf{C})^{1-t}$, where $\mathbf{C}$ is the residual and $t$ is the share of the spectrum that $\mathbf{s}$ carries. It reproduces effective rank to $3\times10^{-15}$ relative error on all 4,743 measurements we hold: Pythia at 160M, 410M and 1.4B over 20 checkpoints, OLMo-2 1B over 13, every layer of each, three corpus blocks, plus a sample-size sweep. Because effective rank normalizes eigenvalues while RankMe normalizes singular values, the two receive different shares, and we prove that RankMe's is never the larger of the two. That ordering decides the damage: at the peak layer of Pythia-1.4B a single direction holds 85 percent of the eigenvalue mass but 6 percent of the singular-value mass, and distorts the two statistics by $243\times$ and $1.24\times$. The direction is the attention sink, which acquires that role during training, ending with roughly half its energy on the $1/512$ of tokens at position 0; its strength alone accounts for 94 to 97 percent of the variance in $E_1$. Projecting it out turns a $105\times$ collapse in effective rank into $2.1\times$ growth, leaves the raw RankMe trajectory pointing the wrong way at two of three scales, and leaves one early transient that OLMo-2 reproduces. OLMo-2 grows no sink over its first 76B tokens, and there the raw and residual metrics differ by a median of 3.5 percent against $32\times$ to $243\times$ in Pythia, which separates the sink from the projection used to study it. Because entropy-based ranks have not converged at 524,288 tokens, we report ratios at a fixed token count and never an absolute rank.
